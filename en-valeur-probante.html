For architects

A decision made by phone,
disputed a year later.

You approved a colour over the phone, a partition moved on site, a material substituted by text message. Twelve months pass, a defect is raised, and nobody remembers the same thing. This page explains what ALBA keeps, what it is worth — and what it is not worth.

What is kept, with every decision

A decision in ALBA is not a message in a thread. It is a dated object that carries with it what is needed to be read again long afterwards.

  • The author — who approved it, with their role on the project at that moment.
  • The server time-stamp — the date and time recorded by our servers, not those of the author's device, which can be set by hand.
  • The exact content of what was submitted, as it was submitted.
  • The attachments, versioned: the approved drawing stays attached to the approval, not to the latest version of the drawing.
  • The thread that preceded it — the questions, the reservations, the back-and-forth that led to the decision.

That last point is the one people underestimate. An isolated approval proves little; an approval accompanied by the question it answers tells the story of a decision.

Electronic signature, and its limits

Decisions are signed electronically within the meaning of the European eIDAS Regulation, at the simple level. In practice: the signatory's identity, the date and the integrity of the content are established and retained as evidence.

What ALBA does not do, and you had better know beforehand

An advanced or qualified signature — the kind required for certain instruments, notably before a notary or for formal public procurement — requires a certification arrangement that we do not provide. For those, use your usual route.

ALBA covers the everyday material: design decisions, client approvals, drawing approvals, site reports. Which is to say, precisely what gets lost today.

We would rather say so here than let you find out on the day it matters. A tool that promised the force of a notarised instrument would expose you more than it protects you.

What it changes in a dispute

Under French law, a matter of fact may be proved by any means (preuve libre): a judge weighs whatever is put before them. What makes the difference is therefore not a stamp — it is the consistency and the anteriority of what you produce.

A message thread exported from a phone is easily contested: nothing in it prevents a deletion, an edit, a device clock set wrong. A record time-stamped by a third party, held in a continuous sequence and exportable as a whole, is far harder to contest.

We will not tell you that ALBA "wins a case". We will tell you that most disputes in architectural practice never get that far: they die the day someone reopens the dated, signed decision with the drawing that came with it. It is the argument that does not happen that saves you time.

Ten years is the real horizon

France's ten-year structural warranty (garantie décennale) does not care about your subscription. Your archives have to stay readable long after the site closes, and possibly after ALBA.

  • Complete export, at any time, in one click: PDF, ZIP, CSV.
  • The formats are open and readable without us: a PDF still opens in ten years, a proprietary message thread does not.
  • The export contains the decisions with their attachments and their time-stamps, not merely a list.
  • Your data stays yours, including if you leave: it is written into the terms, not only on this page.

Without changing the way you work

Traceability is only worth anything if it costs nothing to produce. Nobody fills in a register in the evening. In ALBA the archive is the by-product: you request an approval because you need it, and the evidence builds itself.

Your consultants and your clients take part free of charge — no subscription to take out, no software to install, which is the condition for them to play along. See the page written for them.
